# Nightdrift v0.1.0 — First drive

## Plan

Nightdrift is a browser toy: an endless, slow night drive through a procedurally generated city, accompanied by generative ambient music. Opening the page shows a start screen. Pressing Start begins the drive and requests audio activation. On the normal path, the city and music begin together. If audio activation fails, the visual drive continues with understandable feedback and a retry control. The same seed recreates the same city and score.

This release adds the complete experience using Vite, TypeScript, three.js and the Web Audio API. It has no backend and loads no external runtime assets. Geometry, textures and sound are generated in code.

The workspace contains the original goal at `.factory/goals/2fd2a8ea.md`, the draft at `.factory/drafts/2fd2a8ea-plan.md`, the saved plan at `.factory/plans/035a973a.md`, and fourteen standalone backlog records. All fourteen records have `status: "backlog"`; none is embedded in this plan. There is no application code, `package.json` or `.factory/architecture.md`.

### Behaviour examples

- **Same seed, same experience.** Load `?seed=marlowe`, drive for one active minute, then reload under the same motion settings. Buildings at the same coordinates and events at the same score time match. Case is preserved in seeds; representative fixtures such as `marlowe` and `Marlowe` must demonstrate variation. This does not promise a collision-free mapping from every possible string into a finite hash.
- **Chunk seam.** Crossing from chunk `(0,0)` into `(0,1)`, `(0,-1)` or an east/west neighbour shows continuous streets without cracks, duplicate boundary surfaces or missing block corners.
- **Turn clearance.** Throughout an intersection curve, the camera and its near-plane footprint remain outside building volumes, including after resizing or entering fullscreen.
- **Pause.** Press Space, wait ten seconds, then press Space again. Movement, atmosphere and score resume from their paused positions without advancing through the wait.
- **Hidden page.** Hiding a running page pauses it. Returning leaves it paused until the user resumes. Hiding an already paused page does not change that state.
- **Mute.** Mute during playback. Output after the final master gain becomes silent after the gain ramp and analyser buffer have settled. The slider keeps its position; unmute restores that level.
- **New seed.** Changing seed resets city, route, atmosphere and score to their starting state, updates the seed URL parameter, preserves volume/mute and running/paused state, and leaves no old voices or render loops.
- **WebGL unavailable.** With context acquisition disabled, the page shows readable recovery guidance rather than a blank canvas or uncaught exception.
- **Storage denied.** With storage reads and writes throwing, Start and all controls still work; settings simply do not survive reload.
- **Audio blocked or interrupted.** Visuals continue with a sound status. Retry activates sound at the current score position without replaying missed notes or restarting the drive. Retry while paused leaves playback paused.
- **Fullscreen denied.** A rejected request leaves the experience usable, reports the failure and keeps the fullscreen control consistent with the browser’s actual state.

### Approach

#### Deterministic seed and world data

Treat the first `seed` query value as a case-sensitive decoded string. Do not trim or lowercase an explicit nonempty seed. Generate a seed when the first value is missing or empty. Preserve unrelated parameters and the URL fragment; replacing the seed produces one seed parameter.

Use nondeterministic randomness only to select a new seed. Derive independent deterministic streams for city generation, route decisions and musical parameters. Chunk descriptors depend on seed and integer coordinates, never request order. Score events depend on musical position, never scheduler polling order.

Tests compare structured outputs across reload-equivalent calls, shuffled generation order, negative coordinates and representative different seeds. Include known fixtures so a constant generator cannot pass.

The reproducibility promise concerns descriptors, route decisions and score events. It does not require identical pixels or audio samples across browser implementations.

#### City layout, streaming and rendering

Keep chunk size, road width, building setbacks, intersection dimensions and generation maxima in one shared definition. Use half-open boundary ownership. Buildings remain inside block interiors and roads connect on every edge.

Maintain a 5 × 5 neighbourhood centred on the camera’s chunk. Its 25-chunk limit is a planning default, not an operator-supplied number. Choose fog and far-plane distances from the minimum distance to the loaded boundary as the camera moves within its chunk.

Retain fixed shared instance pools, with capacity derived from the maximum possible contents of 25 chunks. Group visual instances by compatible geometry and material; use the fewest pools that preserve the required appearance. Instancing reduces draw calls, but the actual count depends on the implementation and must be measured. [three.js InstancedMesh documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html)

A transition must fit those pools:

1. Compute the required neighbourhood.
2. Release outgoing chunk slices and records.
3. Reuse their slots for incoming chunks.
4. Finish all instance updates before rendering the new camera state.

No render occurs between release and refill. Allocating incoming chunks before releasing outgoing ones would require extra capacity and contradict the chosen limit.

Unused or released slots must not render stale geometry. Mark changed instance buffers for upload and maintain correct culling bounds after streaming and rebasing. Tests cover replacement of a dense chunk with a sparse one, diagonal crossings and repeated reuse.

Chunk slices are owned by chunks. Shared instance meshes, geometries, materials and generated textures live until application teardown. Dispose each owned resource explicitly; disposing a material does not dispose its textures. Also release instance-specific GPU resources and the renderer at teardown. [three.js disposal guidance](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/how-to-dispose-of-objects.html)

Keep absolute chunk coordinates separate from local rendering coordinates. Rebase camera position, resident instances, path data expressed locally and culling bounds together, without changing procedural identity.

#### Camera, time and atmosphere

Use a deterministic route generator with a bounded queue of upcoming road segments. Construct intersection curves inside the road area, accounting for the camera’s near plane and supported viewport proportions. Safe paths avoid a physics engine or collision-recovery system.

Advance from elapsed active time, not frame count. Account for segment boundaries consistently when the same duration is delivered in different frame sizes. Pause freezes movement, atmosphere and score position. Reset timing anchors on resume so elapsed wall-clock time is not replayed.

Initial tuning defaults are 6 world units per second, halved under reduced motion, and slower traversal of turns. Ease live preference changes while preserving route decisions. Detect the preference using `prefers-reduced-motion`. [MDN reduced-motion reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)

Use a continuous 20-minute dusk–night–dusk cycle with no daylight phase or discontinuity at its wrap. The HUD reads the renderer’s atmosphere state. These values remain implementation defaults.

In normal operation, movement, atmosphere and score share the same pause/reset lifecycle. The test-only speed multiplier accelerates movement, not music or atmosphere; expose the relevant times separately to avoid conflating them.

#### Music interpretation and evidence

Keep the operator’s wording in R5. Do not silently replace it with “no short soundtrack loop.”

**Proposed acceptance interpretation:** the ideal seeded score has no ultimately periodic phrase sequence, with the variation carried by audible musical parameters. Ordinary notes and finite motifs may recur. The browser implements a checked finite range of that construction.

This is an interpretation requiring explicit visibility, not a proved statement about what every listener means. Other meaningful requirements—such as forbidding adjacent repeated phrases—are different and are not automatically implied.

For an infinite sequence over a finite event alphabet, some finite passages necessarily recur. That conditional argument does not establish that every possible musical interpretation is impossible. Conversely, a finite browser execution cannot demonstrate literal non-repetition for infinite time.

Retain Thue–Morse as the candidate construction for T9. The sequence defined by parity of the binary digit sum is not ultimately periodic. [Allouche and Shallit, *The ubiquitous Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence*, §§2–4](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Papers/ubiq15.pdf)

The implementation must preserve that property through its musical mapping:

- Use a fixed phrase duration. Compute the symbol from the absolute phrase index without wrapping the index.
- Combine it with a fixed seed-derived bit, such as choosing the sequence or its complement. An unspecified time-varying combination could cancel the variation.
- Give the two symbols audibly distinct, harmonically compatible treatments.
- Ensure the symbol remains recoverable from each phrase’s normalized musical events across every permitted chord and parameter choice. Pairwise differences under one chord are insufficient if other transformations make treatments indistinguishable.
- Keep IDs and absolute timestamps out of the distinction used as evidence.

The design argument is conditional: if phrase events became ultimately periodic, a fixed decoder of those events would yield an ultimately periodic symbol sequence, contradicting the construction. This establishes the intended property of the ideal phrase-event model. It does not prove perpetual browser operation, perceptual novelty or musical quality.

Keep the argument and assumptions beside `src/score.ts`. Unit tests verify the implementation against them; listening checks verify that synthesis preserves the intended distinction and mood. Neither finite samples nor unique metadata prove infinite non-periodicity.

Use `number` indices as already planned; do not add `BigInt` without a demonstrated need. Avoid JavaScript bitwise operations that truncate large indices to 32 bits. Test values around 32-bit boundaries and near the safe-integer limit.

An integral phrase index alone does not guarantee accurate event timestamps. T9 must define and test the supported range of all time arithmetic, including phrase offsets, interval endpoints and successor indices. Reject unsupported or non-finite inputs without wrapping or hanging. The earlier billion-year phrase-index estimate is not an end-to-end timing guarantee.

Q1 records the outstanding acceptance interpretation. Foundation and the candidate construction can proceed without pretending that the operator has already answered it.

#### Pure score and audio graph

Represent the score as a pure interval query over seed and musical time. Onsets use half-open intervals: adjacent interval results concatenate to the same ordered events as one combined interval. Provide sustained-note state separately, allowing resume inside a note without duplicating its onset.

Keep harmony, instrument parameters and duration rules in data. Generate pad, arpeggio and hum without downloaded samples. Generated noise uses a deterministic source.

Create or resume the AudioContext directly inside Start or Retry’s user gesture, before awaiting unrelated work. Foundation establishes a silent unlocked graph; Sound adds instruments. Activation can still fail and must be handled. [MDN Web Audio best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices)

Use bounded look-ahead scheduling, bounded voices and explicit stop/disconnect. Every layer and effect routes through one final master gain, with short ramps for mute, volume and stopping.

Pause cancels future scheduled playback, silences active voices and freezes score position. Resume reconstructs sustained notes with their remaining duration and envelope position. A late scheduler skips expired onsets and reconstructs current sustained state; it must not emit a burst of missed notes.

Audio failure does not stop visual time. A later successful retry joins the current score position. Handle unexpected suspension or closure with status and recovery. Repeated Start/Retry clicks and stale asynchronous completions after pause, seed change or teardown must not create duplicate graphs or restart obsolete playback.

Stop timers, listeners, render callbacks and voices during teardown, and close the application-owned audio context.

#### Interface and failure behaviour

New seed resets city, route, atmosphere and score to time zero while preserving volume, mute and running/paused state. It must not start a paused session or implicitly retry failed audio.

Persist volume and mute in one versioned localStorage record. Validate types, finite numeric values, ranges and record version. Survive malformed JSON and read/write failures. Apply settings before the first audible output. Do not persist fullscreen, autoplay permission or an active session.

Space toggles pause when focus is on the page or scene, ignoring key-repeat and preventing page scrolling. Focused controls retain their own keyboard behaviour. A button activation must not also trigger the global pause handler. About supports Escape and returns focus to its opener; if modal, it keeps focus within the dialog while open.

Fullscreen starts from a user gesture, reflects actual state including browser Escape, and handles unsupported APIs or rejected requests. Resizing and fullscreen changes update the projection and retain camera clearance.

The chosen three.js renderer requires WebGL 2; WebGL 1 support was removed in r163. Probe context availability and show the fallback before attempting renderer initialization with an unavailable context. Context loss stops movement and sound, clears readiness and offers reload. [three.js WebGLRenderer documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html)
